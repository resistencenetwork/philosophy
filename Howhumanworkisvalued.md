**The network could give verified human creation a premium through greater visibility, creator-controlled prices, and clear evidence of how the work was made.** That makes supporting human creativity an explicit choice across participating apps.

I’d start with your three labels, using these proposed definitions.

| Label | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Human-generated** | People produce the substantive writing, images, music, or performance. Routine tools such as spellcheck and automatic captions are allowed. | Someone films themselves cooking and explaining their recipe. |
| **AI-assisted** | A person makes a substantial creative contribution, and generative AI supplies meaningful parts of the finished work. | A person writes and presents a lesson containing AI-generated illustrations. |
| **AI-generated** | Generative AI produces most of the substantive material, with a person prompting, selecting, or publishing it. | A video with an AI-written script, synthetic presenter, and generated scenes. |

These definitions would need community agreement, particularly around translation, accessibility tools, and mixed productions. A short disclosure could explain exactly where AI was used.

**Each upload should also show whether its label is self-declared or verified.** A registered human is accountable for an upload. Verification examines the creation of that particular work.

Network verifiers could review evidence appropriate to the content.

- **Videos and photographs** could have original recordings, production files, and editing history.
- **Writing** could have drafts, research notes, and revision history.
- **Music and artwork** could have recordings, sketches, project files, and intermediate versions.

The creator would grant reviewers limited access to this evidence. It would remain under the creator’s control, with separate permission required for any further use. An approved label would identify the reviewed version and what evidence supported the decision.

There is a practical limitation here. AI detectors have variable accuracy, so a detector score would provide supporting evidence. NIST’s text evaluation found that some generators could fool most tested detectors. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/publications/2024-nist-genai-pilot-study-text-text-evaluation-overview-and-results?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Signed creation records such as C2PA Content Credentials could help preserve a file’s recorded history. Their reliability still depends on the source making those claims and the completeness of that history. **Priority and price would work together, with different responsibilities.**

| Mechanism | Proposed rule |
|---|---|
| **Recommendations** | Among relevant works of comparable quality, give verified human content a ranking advantage. |
| **Viewer choice** | Offer human-only, human-and-assisted, and all-content feeds. |
| **Pricing** | Let verified creators offer premium prices and join collections dedicated to human work. |
| **Discovery** | Give emerging human creators opportunities to reach audiences while they build a record. |

For example, a human creator could offer a verified cooking lesson for **₹5**, with the app displaying the agreed service fee and creator’s share before purchase. Viewers would know they are paying for someone’s demonstrated work and experience. Whether ₹5 attracts buyers would depend on the lesson’s value to that audience.

**Verifiers would need accountability too.** I’d pay them for completing an evidence-based review, with payment independent of approval, and provide appeals and occasional second reviews. Community funding could keep verification affordable. Otherwise, the ability to pay for verification could determine who receives visibility.

The badge would establish the reviewed production method. Audience feedback and other quality checks would assess whether the work is useful, accurate, or entertaining.
