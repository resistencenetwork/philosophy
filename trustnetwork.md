# The Trust Network

**Of the community, by the community, for the community.**

Most people depend on companies for a paycheck. That makes it hard to bargain as an individual, especially when jobs are scarce. But companies depend on people too. They need us to pay attention to what they offer, and they need us to choose to buy it.

Think about what happens when you open a video app. A company pays the app to put an ad in front of you. The app decides when to show it, and information about what you watch or click can help it decide what to show next. AI can make those decisions more personal and give the companies running the biggest apps even more influence over what people see and buy.

**The Trust Network asks a simple question. What if people could set the terms on which companies get access to their attention and information?**

### You decide what your attention is worth

Say you’re happy to watch short videos about new technology if you get paid at least ₹2 a minute. You set that rate once. You can choose the topics you’re interested in, turn offers off whenever you want, and skip any offer that comes through.

Now imagine opening a video app and seeing two videos.

- **Pay ₹2 to watch a cooking tutorial.** You want the recipe, so you pay the creator.
- **Earn ₹1 to watch a 30-second product demo.** The company wants your time, so it pays you. You still decide whether to buy the product.

These tiny payments are called **microtransactions**. The app can add them to a running balance so you don’t have to make a separate bank transfer every time you watch a video.

### You join once and use different apps

There would be one sign-up service owned and run by the community. Members would vote on the shared rules. Independent groups could build their own video, social, shopping, or work apps. You could move between those apps using the same membership and keep the preferences you’ve already set.

Your personal information stays under your control. If a company wants your viewing history for research, it tells you what it wants, why, and for how long. You can agree, name a price, or decline. You can choose where to keep your data. If the community provides storage and backups, you pay it for that service.

Companies can register and do business under the community’s rules. AI agents, which are programs that can do tasks on someone’s behalf, also have to register before accessing resources in the network. If a company sends an AI agent to gather information, the network knows which company is responsible. The agent needs permission and pays any agreed charges, just as the company would.

### People can bargain together

Suppose 500 families want rooftop solar. Each family could approach installers individually. Through the network, they could tell installers there are 500 interested households and ask them to compete on price, installation quality, and warranty. Each family still chooses whether to buy. Together, they have more bargaining power because companies are competing for business the families organized themselves.

The same principle applies to work. People can join with or without formal qualifications, choose the work they want to take on, and have somewhere to turn if a company fails to pay or an assignment is unsafe.

### Where AI and jobs fit in

AI can do useful work and help companies become more productive. When that also leads to job losses, people should have a voice in how the gains are shared and how workers are supported through the change. A few rupees for watching a video address one transaction. **The ability to meet basic needs and live with dignity is a broader commitment that should apply to everyone.**

Companies can still build products, compete, and earn profits. The change is that people can organize across apps, set the terms for their own participation, and have a say in the rules companies and AI agents follow when dealing with them.
